Guide

Write apps for BMW iDrive

An open-source SDK and a free compiler for the NBT Evo head unit. Your app runs on your desktop first; the same code builds for the car, where NBT Manager installs, supervises and updates it.

What the SDK gives an app

  • The screen: a full-screen OpenGL ES 2 surface, or an LVGL interface.
  • Input: the iDrive controller and its buttons, USB gamepads by pad family.
  • Sound through the car's speakers, mixed with other apps.
  • Files sent from the phone or imported from USB, settings changed from the phone while the app runs, a status line.
  • The car, with the user's permission: live readings, engine data and fault codes, the network.
  • A phone page: an app can bring its own screen to the phone app.
  • Emulators: a libretro host, so a core becomes an app with a game list, saves and a pause menu.

Start

  • Open the template (a GitHub template repository) or the examples.
  • Download the toolchain: Clang and LLD for Linux, macOS and Windows. Nothing to license.
  • Build and run on your desktop; no car needed until you want to try it there.

With a coding agent

The SDK ships skills that teach a coding agent the SDK, the controls, packaging and tests. Open the template, start your agent and describe what you want on the screen. To port a game or emulator, point it at the source: the porting guide is the path Doom and four emulators took.

Publish

  • In Get apps: open a pull request on community-apps. We build your app from its source, sign it and list it.
  • On your own car: sign with your own developer key, added from the phone (coming with the next NBT Manager release).
