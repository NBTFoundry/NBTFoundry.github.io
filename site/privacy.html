Privacy

NBT Manager for Android (org.nbtfoundry.manager). Updated November 28, 2026.

No account, no advertising

The app has no account and no advertising. The only data NBTFoundry receives from it is its analytics (below), which one switch turns off.

One thing comes from Google: the QR scanner is Google's ML Kit, which sends Google device information (manufacturer, model, Android version) for its diagnostics and usage statistics, over HTTPS. Google does not pass it on (ML Kit's data disclosure).

Analytics, which you can turn off

To find and fix problems, the app sends Expo (the service it is built with) how long it takes to start, its errors and its crashes, with the phone's model, the Android version, the app's version and a random number that stands for this installation. NBTFoundry reads them there. Nothing about your car, your applications or your files is in them. More › Settings › Analytics turns this off at any time, and deletes what was not sent yet (Expo's privacy policy).

Kept on your phone

  • Your paired cars: their names and addresses, and the phone's signing key for each, which stays in the Android Keystore and never leaves the phone.
  • The application packages you downloaded, and your settings.
  • Logs and crash reports you read from a car. They leave the phone only when you share them.

Uninstalling the app deletes all of it. The app is excluded from Android backups.

Connections

  • Your car, over its own Wi-Fi, when you pair, read, install or change something.
  • GitHub, when the app opens, when you check for new versions in Apps or download one: the catalog from nbtfoundry.github.io and packages from github.com. GitHub sees your IP address, as for any web request (GitHub's privacy statement).
  • Expo, when the app looks for an update of itself, which it does by itself when it opens or comes back to the front: the app's version and the random number of this installation, so that Expo can answer with the right update. With analytics on, also what is described above.
  • Google, after you use the QR scanner: ML Kit's device information, above.

The app makes no other connection. It keeps itself up to date: it downloads its own updates and starts them the next time it opens, with no switch. When it opens, it also looks for new versions of your applications. Connecting to your car when it opens is off until you turn it on. Everything else, apart from analytics, follows something you do.

Permissions

  • Camera: only to scan the Wi-Fi code on the car's screen. No picture is stored or sent.
  • Wi-Fi and nearby devices: to join the car's Wi-Fi. On Android 12 and earlier, Android requires the location permission for this; the app never reads your location.
